An interactive learning app, built with a short course on how the periodic table was made. Instead of memorizing the table, the student repeats the steps that produced it. [Open the app](/periodic-table/) or [open it full screen](/assets/apps/periodic-table/index.html).

## The five steps

1. **Elements (1789).** Sort Lavoisier's 33 "simple substances" into elements, compounds and things that are not matter, and see why he weighed everything in a closed vessel.
2. **Weight order (1864).** Put the lightest elements in order of atomic weight and slide the row length until each chemical family lines up in a column. Newlands found the rhythm at seven.
3. **Gaps (1869).** Predict the missing elements gallium and germanium by averaging their neighbours, then compare with what was found.
4. **Atomic number (1913).** Enter an X-ray energy and read off the atomic number with Moseley's law. Three pairs that atomic weight puts in the wrong order come out right.
5. **Shells (1920s).** Add electrons one at a time into the lowest free subshell and watch the rows of 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32 appear.

## Sources and limits

- Lavoisier's table: *Traité élémentaire de chimie* (1789). K-alpha line energies: X-ray Data Booklet, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Table 1-2. Atomic weights are rounded IUPAC abridged values.
- Years and discoverers shown in the app are approximate. They come from general knowledge and should be checked against a history of the periodic table before they are quoted.
- Stage 5 uses the simple filling order and marks the measured exceptions (for example chromium and copper); the exceptions list was written from general knowledge and should also be checked.
