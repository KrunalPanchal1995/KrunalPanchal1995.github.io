Under the hood

The solver behind the flow

The hero is a 2D incompressible flow solved on the GPU every frame (WebGL2, stable-fluids scheme). Below: the equations, the matrix its pressure step inverts, and live numbers from the running simulation.

1. The equations

Incompressible Navier-Stokes for velocity u and pressure p:

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = -∇p/ρ + ν∇²u + f,  ∇·u = 0

The solver advances one time step in three stages, in this order: force (vorticity confinement, buoyancy, inflow, your pointer), project (make the field divergence-free), advect (MacCormack semi-Lagrangian). There is no explicit ν∇²u term: viscosity here is the numerical diffusion of the advection step plus a small linear decay.

u* = u_n + Δt f
∇²p = (ρ/Δt) ∇·u*,  u = u* - (Δt/ρ) ∇p
u(x, t+Δt) ≈ u(x - Δt u, t)

On the grid (spacing h) the Laplacian is the 5-point stencil, one linear equation per cell:

(p(i+1,j) + p(i-1,j) + p(i,j+1) + p(i,j-1) - 4p(i,j)) / h² = b(i,j)
b(i,j) = (ρ/Δt) (u*(i+1,j) - u*(i-1,j) + v*(i,j+1) - v*(i,j-1)) / 2h

2. The matrix it solves

Stacking all cells gives a sparse linear system A p = b. Each row has at most five nonzeros: the diagonal and the four neighbours (+1). Hero boundaries: left, top and bottom are zero-gradient walls (the missing neighbour is replaced by the cell itself, so the diagonal becomes -3 or -2); the right edge is an outlet with p = 0 (diagonal stays -4, no right neighbour).

Hover or focus the matrix (arrow keys) to read an entry.

  • diagonal (-4, -3, -2)
  • neighbour (+1)
  • zero

The solver does not factorise A. It runs a fixed number of Jacobi sweeps per frame (see iterations below), warm-started from last frame's pressure times 0.8. In the shader the units are h = 1 and p is scaled by Δt/(ρh), so ρ and Δt drop out:

p_new = (p_L + p_R + p_B + p_T - b) / 4
b = ½(u_R - u_L + v_T - v_B)

With general spacing the update is p_new = (p_L + p_R + p_B + p_T - h²b)/4. At a wall the shader substitutes the cell's own value for the missing neighbour, so those rows iterate p ← p - (b - A p)/4. After the sweeps the velocity is corrected with a central difference:

u ← u - ½(p_R - p_L, p_T - p_B)

The cylinder and the hot burner act as extra zero-gradient interior walls; the demo matrix above leaves them out.

What each number means
Sim grid
Cells of the velocity and pressure grid (width × height). The picture is drawn at a higher resolution than this.
Quality tier
0 is highest, 3 lowest. The hero lowers it automatically if frames take too long.
Jacobi sweeps / frame
How many times the pressure update above is applied each frame. More sweeps leave less divergence.
Frames per second, frame interval
Measured between browser animation frames, so it is capped by the display rate. It is not GPU time.
Time step Δt
Seconds advanced per step: the frame interval, clamped to 1/120 - 1/30 s.
CFL number
Δt · max|u| / h, the number of cells the fastest fluid crosses in one step, using the speed after the solve. Semi-Lagrangian advection stays stable above 1.
max |∇·u| before / after
Largest divergence of the velocity field over the whole grid (boundary cells included), in 1/s: the field entering the pressure solve versus the field leaving it, sampled once a second from the GPU in 8 × 8 cell blocks. After is not zero: a fixed number of sweeps solves A p = b only approximately. Advection, applied afterwards, is not included.

Numbers shown come from the solver in this tab; nothing is sent anywhere.

Overview

Research at a glance

  • Combustion Kinetics & Mechanism Reduction — mechanism development, validation, and reduction of detailed kinetic models to tractable, simulation-ready form
  • Experimental & Numerical Combustion — pool-flame characterization and downdraft gasification alongside CFD and chemical-kinetics simulation of the same systems
  • Fair Multi-Objective Optimization & Uncertainty Quantification — multi-stage, multi-dataset calibration of Arrhenius parameters with correlated-parameter Bayesian UQ, built around MOSIAC, my open-source optimization framework
  • Reduced-Order Modelling & Scientific ML — neural and response-surface surrogates that stand in for expensive stiff simulations
  • Gasification — steam/catalyst-assisted downdraft fixed-bed gasification, from rig commissioning to syngas analysis
  • CFD — PANSOPHIA, an in-house CFD/multiphysics solver, and reacting-flow and rig-design studies built on top of it
  • Optimization & Reinforcement Learning — dokimazo, an open-source, dependency-light optimization/RL workbench with a plug-and-play graph IR — closed with an RL agent that tunes a real molecule’s rotatable bonds to find its lowest-energy 3D shape

The full research overview — themes, methods, experiments, and publications — is on the Research page.
